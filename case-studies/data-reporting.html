THE PRINCIPLE

Collecting data is not the same as creating insight.

Across both Customer Success and Program Operations roles, I have repeatedly encountered the same challenge: organizations often have substantial amounts of data, but the information is not structured in a way that makes decisions easier.

My approach is to begin with the business question—not the dashboard.

I identify what teams and leaders need to understand, determine what information can answer those questions, improve how that information is captured, and then build reporting around the resulting data.

Business Question → Structured Data → Reporting → Insight → Action
01

GITHUB EDUCATION

Moving operational tracking beyond spreadsheets.

Program activity relied heavily on spreadsheet-based tracking, which made it difficult to consistently surface trends, recurring themes, and overall program status.

I transitioned this work into a structured Microsoft Lists / SharePoint workflow and connected the resulting data to a Power BI dashboard.

SALESFORCE REPORTING

Using data quality to protect the customer experience.

Existing Salesforce renewal reporting could miss institutions approaching renewal.

That was not simply a reporting inconvenience. Missing an institution could contribute to a gap in service, which could then become a customer escalation.

I taught myself the necessary Salesforce reporting functionality and created a more reliable report for identifying institutions requiring renewal review.

THE CONNECTION

Data quality → operational reliability → customer experience
03

CORL TECHNOLOGIES

Building meaningful customer health reporting.

As CORL developed its Customer Success function, I researched approaches to customer health and evaluated how existing systems such as HubSpot and Power BI could support a more structured view of customer engagement and risk.

But defining customer health introduced another question: Were we measuring the things customers themselves actually valued?

When developing the QBR framework, I recommended asking customers directly what information and outcomes were meaningful to them.

When appropriate, those insights could then influence what the organization tracked and reported, creating a stronger connection between internal metrics and customer-defined value.

04

FROM REPORTING TO ACTION

A dashboard should change what happens next.

Whether I am looking at customer health, renewal activity, support operations, or broader program performance, my goal is not simply to visualize data.

Reporting should help answer questions such as:

01

Where is risk emerging?

02

What trends are changing?

03

Where is the process creating friction?

04

Which customers or program areas need attention?

05

Are our interventions working?

06

What should we do differently next?

MY APPROACH

Start with the decision. Then build the data system that helps people make it.

Across different teams, platforms, and business problems, I have used the same underlying approach: understand the question, improve the quality and structure of the data, surface meaningful patterns, and connect those insights to action.
