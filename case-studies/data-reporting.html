01

My Reporting Philosophy

Reporting is only useful when it helps someone make a better decision.

Across both program operations and customer success, I have focused on improving the path between raw operational information and meaningful action.

Business Question → Structured Data → Reporting → Insight → Action
02

Modernizing Operational Tracking

At GitHub Education, operational tracking relied in part on spreadsheet-based processes that created opportunities for stronger structure and visibility.

BEFORE

Spreadsheet-Based Tracking

Information maintained across worksheets required additional manual effort to organize, interpret, and report.

AFTER

Structured Reporting System

Transitioned operational tracking into Microsoft Lists and SharePoint and connected the structured information to Power BI reporting.

03

Improving Salesforce Reporting

Reliable operational decisions depend on reliable source data.

When an existing Salesforce renewal report did not consistently capture the institutions needed for operational planning, I taught myself the reporting structure and developed a more reliable way to surface the required information.

I also worked to improve consistency in renewal notes and issue documentation, making the underlying data easier to interpret and use.

The goal: improve both the reporting layer and the quality of the operational information feeding it.
04

Connecting Customer Data to Customer Strategy

At CORL Technologies, reporting was closely tied to customer health, retention, engagement, and executive communication.

Customer Health

Developed reporting approaches designed to help the team better understand customer engagement, relationship health, and potential risk.

Executive Reporting

Created structured reporting for customer and internal leadership conversations, including QBRs and strategic account discussions.

Customer-Defined Value

Built reporting conversations around the information customers actually considered meaningful rather than assuming which metrics mattered most.

CRM Visibility

Used CRM and operational data to create stronger visibility into lifecycle activity and customer needs.

05

Turning Reporting Into Action

The final step is the one that matters most: deciding what to do with the information.

What changed? Identify meaningful movement in the data.
Why did it change? Connect trends to operational or customer context.
What needs attention? Surface risks, bottlenecks, and opportunities.
What should happen next? Translate insight into ownership and action.

THE TAKEAWAY

Data becomes valuable when it creates clarity and action.

I approach reporting as part of the operating system of a program: structure the information, make it trustworthy, surface what matters, and connect the insight to a decision.
