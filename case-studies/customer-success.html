THE OPPORTUNITY

Building the systems behind strong customer relationships.

As a founding member of CORL's Customer Success team, I had the opportunity to help define how the organization would understand customer health, identify retention risk, measure engagement, and communicate value to clients.

Rather than inheriting a mature Customer Success operating model, I helped research and develop many of the foundational practices the new function needed.

01

CUSTOMER HEALTH

Defining what a healthy customer relationship looks like.

I researched Customer Success use cases and practices from other organizations to understand how established CS functions evaluated customer health.

I then translated those concepts into recommendations appropriate for CORL's environment, including ways our existing tools—such as HubSpot and Power BI—could support customer health tracking.

I also evaluated where existing tooling had limitations and researched dedicated Customer Success technology that could strengthen the model.

Research → Define signals → Evaluate tooling → Track health → Take action
02

TECHNOLOGY EVALUATION

Evaluating technology around the process—not the other way around.

As part of the customer health initiative, I piloted the implementation of ChurnZero to evaluate whether a dedicated Customer Success platform could improve visibility into engagement, health, lifecycle activity, and retention risk.

The objective was not simply to introduce another platform. I wanted to understand what information the team actually needed and determine whether existing systems or specialized technology were best positioned to provide it.

ChurnZero HubSpot Power BI Technology Evaluation
03

QBR STRATEGY

Creating a dedicated conversation about outcomes and value.

Customer health conversations and discussions about the state of the customer's contract had previously been combined.

I recommended separating these conversations and created a formal Quarterly Business Review framework that provided customers and their delivery teams with a dedicated opportunity to discuss progress, risk reduction, outcomes, priorities, and demonstrated value.

KEY INSIGHT

We could not demonstrate value effectively if we were not measuring what customers themselves considered valuable.

I incorporated an important question into the process: What data and outcomes does the customer find meaningful?

When appropriate, those answers could then inform what we tracked going forward, allowing future reviews to connect internal reporting with customer-defined value.

BEFORE

Combined Conversation

Health + contract status + operational discussion within the same touchpoint.

AFTER

Strategic QBR

Dedicated review of outcomes, progress, risk, priorities, and customer-defined value.

04

RETENTION STRATEGY

Turning customer risk into structured action.

I piloted a customer retention strategy designed around recurring risks we observed in customer behavior and relationships.

The objective was to create a repeatable playbook that helped the team move from recognizing that an account was at risk to determining what should happen next.

01

Identify Risk

Recognize behavioral or relationship indicators.

02

Understand

Determine the underlying concern or source of friction.

03

Correct

Create an actionable corrective action plan.

04

Remediate

Address the customer's immediate concerns.

05

Exceed

Identify opportunities to go beyond remediation.

06

Monitor

Evaluate whether customer health improves.

05

ORGANIZATIONAL CHANGE

Scaling the model through a changing organization.

As leadership and organizational priorities evolved, Customer Success transitioned into a broader Customer Experience function.

I moved into a senior role and helped train associates on customer lifecycle processes, health practices, strategic reviews, risk identification, retention, escalation management, and customer communication.

When workforce reductions required customer portfolios to transition to new owners, I helped associates take ownership of those relationships and became an escalation point when customers experienced issues.

The goal was to preserve continuity for customers while helping newly assigned team members become effective as quickly as possible.

BUSINESS IMPACT

30%

reduction in client churn.

The organization experienced reduced churn and stronger customer engagement as more structured customer-health, QBR, engagement, and retention practices were introduced.

Customer data is only valuable when it leads to action.

My approach connected customer behavior, meaningful data, direct feedback, risk identification, structured action, and measurable outcomes into a repeatable Customer Success operating model.
